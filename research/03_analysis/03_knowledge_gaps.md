# Knowledge Gaps in Marine Plastic Pollution Education Research

## Technical Development Gaps

### Framework Performance for Educational Content
1. **Gap**: Limited comparative studies on how different mobile development frameworks (Flutter vs. React Native) specifically perform for educational content with heavy animations and interactive elements.
2. **Impact**: Uncertainty about which framework would best serve the needs of an educational app about marine plastic pollution during a time-constrained hackathon.
3. **Research Need**: More targeted benchmarking of educational apps built with different frameworks, focusing on animation performance, state management for educational games, and development speed.

### Rapid Prototyping for Educational Games
1. **Gap**: Insufficient guidance on prioritizing features when developing educational games under extreme time constraints (like a 2-hour hackathon).
2. **Impact**: Potential misallocation of limited development time, leading to incomplete or ineffective educational experiences.
3. **Research Need**: Best practices and templates specifically designed for rapid educational game development, with focus on which game mechanics can be implemented quickly while maintaining educational value.

## Educational Content Gaps

### Age-Specific Plastic Pollution Education
1. **Gap**: Limited research on how understanding of plastic pollution concepts varies by specific age groups and how to optimize content accordingly.
2. **Impact**: Risk of creating content that is either too complex for younger users or too simplistic for older users.
3. **Research Need**: More granular studies on comprehension and engagement with plastic pollution concepts across different age groups (6-8, 9-12, 13-15, 16+).

### Long-term Behavioral Impact
1. **Gap**: Insufficient longitudinal studies measuring how digital environmental education translates to real-world behavior change in children.
2. **Impact**: Uncertainty about which digital educational approaches actually lead to meaningful plastic reduction behaviors.
3. **Research Need**: Long-term studies tracking behavioral changes following different approaches to digital environmental education.

## User Experience Gaps

### Optimal Educational Game Mechanics
1. **Gap**: Limited understanding of which game mechanics most effectively teach environmental concepts while maintaining engagement.
2. **Impact**: Risk of creating games that are either engaging but lack educational value or educational but not engaging.
3. **Research Need**: Comparative studies of different game mechanics (collection, simulation, puzzle, etc.) and their effectiveness for teaching specific environmental concepts.

### Balancing Entertainment and Education
1. **Gap**: Insufficient guidance on the optimal ratio of educational content to entertainment elements in environmental education apps.
2. **Impact**: Potential to create either overly didactic apps that lose user interest or entertaining apps with minimal educational impact.
3. **Research Need**: Studies examining engagement and knowledge retention based on different educational-to-entertainment ratios.

## Content Strategy Gaps

### Cultural Relevance in Environmental Education
1. **Gap**: Limited research on how cultural context affects reception of marine pollution education content.
2. **Impact**: Risk of creating content that lacks relevance or resonance for diverse user groups.
3. **Research Need**: Studies on how different cultural contexts affect perception of marine pollution issues and educational approaches that work across cultures.

### Effective Visual Representation of Microplastics
1. **Gap**: Insufficient guidance on how to effectively visualize microscopic plastic pollution in an engaging, educational, and scientifically accurate way.
2. **Impact**: Difficulty in helping users understand a critical but invisible aspect of plastic pollution.
3. **Research Need**: Research on visualization techniques that accurately represent microplastic pollution while maintaining scientific integrity.

## Implementation Strategy Gaps

### Testing Educational Effectiveness in Mobile Apps
1. **Gap**: Limited methodologies for quickly assessing the educational effectiveness of mobile apps, particularly during rapid development cycles.
2. **Impact**: Difficulty in determining whether an educational app is actually achieving its learning objectives.
3. **Research Need**: Development of quick testing protocols specifically designed to measure educational effectiveness of environmental mobile apps.

### Optimal Progression Systems
1. **Gap**: Insufficient research on optimal difficulty progression systems for environmental education games.
2. **Impact**: Risk of creating progression systems that are either too steep (causing frustration) or too gradual (causing boredom).
3. **Research Need**: Studies examining different progression systems and their impact on continued engagement and learning outcomes in environmental education apps.
