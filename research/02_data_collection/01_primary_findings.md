# Primary Findings on Marine Plastic Pollution

## Current State of Plastic Pollution

### Global Statistics

- Approximately 11 billion kilograms of plastic waste enters the ocean annually (Plastic Bank, 2023)
- 75 to 199 billion kilograms of plastic is currently in the ocean (UNEP, 2022)
- By 2050, plastic is expected to outweigh all fish in the sea (Center for Biological Diversity)
- 40% of the world's ocean surfaces now contain plastic pollution in swirling convergences (Center for Biological Diversity)
- Plastic production is slated to increase by 40% over the next decade due to fossil fuel industry expansion (Center for Biological Diversity)

### Persistence of Plastic Waste

- EPA reports that "every bit of plastic ever made still exists" in some form (Center for Biological Diversity)
- Plastic doesn't biodegrade in the environment; it only breaks down into smaller fragments called microplastics
- The Great Pacific Garbage Patch is the largest accumulation of plastic in the world (Center for Biological Diversity)

## Impact on Marine Wildlife

### Scope of the Problem

- Approximately 100,000 marine animals die each year from plastic ingestion or entanglement (Plastic Bank, 2023)
- About 88% of sea creatures across the globe have been negatively impacted by plastic pollution (World Economic Forum, 2022)
- Nearly 700 species of marine animals have been documented eating or getting caught in plastic litter (Center for Biological Diversity)
- Endangered species like Hawaiian monk seals and Pacific loggerhead sea turtles are among the most impacted

### Specifically Affected Marine Species

#### Seabirds

- Scientists have discovered a new disease called "plasticosis" — plastic-induced fibrosis that causes scarring and inflammation in the digestive tract of seabirds
- The disease causes multiorgan damage in affected birds
- Research indicates that 60% of all seabird species have eaten pieces of plastic
- Projected to increase to 99.8% of seabirds by 2050 if current trends continue
- Seabirds suffer from:
  - Dehydration due to blockage in their digestive system
  - Slowed metabolism
  - Low calcium levels in their blood
  - High cholesterol and uric acid
  - Death

#### Sea Turtles

- Approximately 1,000 marine turtles die annually after becoming entangled in plastic debris (likely a significant underestimate)
- 52% of the world's sea turtles have ingested plastic
- A 22% mortality rate from ingesting just a single piece of plastic
- Strong preference for clear, sheet plastic items like plastic bags, which they mistake for jellyfish
- Specific consequences include:
  - Internal damage
  - Malnutrition
  - Increased buoyancy (preventing diving for food)
  - Reduced growth rates
  - Decreased reproductive output

#### Whales

- Multiple documented cases of deceased whales found with large amounts of plastic in their stomachs
  - One young whale in the Philippines had nearly 40 kilograms of plastic in its stomach
- Blue whales estimated to eat more than 10 million pieces of microplastic per day
- Humpback whales ingest approximately 200,000 pieces of microplastic per day
- Common ingested items include balloons, which can lodge in digestive tracts and inhibit feeding
- Plastic entanglement can cause severe injuries

#### Corals

- Plastic waste can smother corals, preventing them from receiving necessary sunlight and oxygen
- Plastic transfers harmful bacteria and pathogens to coral surfaces
- Can cause diseases such as black band disease, which eats away coral tissue
- Plastic has been found contaminating deep-sea cold-water corals and hydrothermal vent communities

#### Fish

- North Pacific fish ingest 12,000 to 24,000 tons of plastic each year
- Plastic ingestion causes intestinal injury and death
- Transfers plastic up the food chain to larger marine species and human seafood consumers
- A recent study found that 25% of fish at markets in California contained plastic in their guts, mostly in the form of microfibers

## Primary Mechanisms of Harm

### Ingestion

- Gastrointestinal blockages leading to starvation
- Transfer of toxic chemicals that leach from plastic
- Internal injuries from sharp edges of broken plastic
- False sense of fullness leading to malnutrition

### Entanglement

- Restricted movement leading to drowning in air-breathing marine animals
- Loss of limbs
- Restricted growth (especially in growing animals)
- Inability to escape predators
- Inability to surface for air (for mammals like whales and seals)

### Habitat Degradation

- Smothering of coral reefs and seafloor ecosystems
- Introduction of invasive species that hitchhike on floating plastic
- Introduction of pathogens and diseases
- Alteration of marine habitats and ecosystems
